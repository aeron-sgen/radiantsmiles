## §1 — Voice register

- **Tone tags:** plainspoken · warm · specific · unhurried · clinical-but-human
- **Person / address:** second person to the patient ("you", "your smile"); first-person plural for the practice ("we", "our team"). Never third-person about ourselves ("Radiant Smiles believes...").
- **Sentence rules:** sentences of 24 words or fewer wherever possible. Lead with the concrete noun or verb. One idea per sentence. No exclamation points. No emoji.
- **Number and claim style:** every number carries its source and its scope inline. "4.5 stars from 506 patient reviews at our Lake Mead office" beats "hundreds of happy patients". If a number cannot carry a source, it does not ship.
- **The governing rule — replace every superlative with a specific.** This is the single voice change that fixes the credibility problem inherited from the current site. Not "award-winning" but "23 years in Las Vegas". Not "the most advanced technology" but the name of the device. Not "top-rated" but the actual rating and where it comes from.
- **Forbidden tone moves:** hype superlatives · fake urgency · pressure countdown devices · unattributed praise · claiming a competitor is worse · medical guarantees of outcome.
- **Reading level:** aim for grade 8 or below. Patients read this while anxious.

## §6 — Scope discipline on the review record

The strongest proof asset carries the strictest handling rule.

**4.5 stars from 506 reviews is the Birdeye record for the Lake Mead office**, fetched 2026-07-30. It is not a practice-wide total. Three ways to use it honestly:

1. State the scope: "4.5 stars from 506 reviews at our Lake Mead office"
2. Ask the owner for the practice-wide figure and cite that instead
3. Cite per-office review counts on each location page, from a platform the owner nominates

Never aggregate, round up, or drop the scope. The whole positioning rests on being the practice that does not overstate, so the proof asset must be the cleanest thing on the site.
