## §2 - pain-it-kills  [M]
required IDs: D-INCUMBENT-007

Sometimes a tooth breaks off close to the gum. Sometimes decay runs in under the edge of it. Either way, what is left above the gumline is too short to hold a crown.

A crown needs a rim of solid tooth to grip. Without that rim it works loose, leaks at the margin, or comes away in food.

Crown lengthening buys that rim back. In some cases reshaping the gum exposes enough tooth. In other cases a small amount of bone is removed as well.

Coverage is the other half of the picture. The ADA Health Policy Institute reports that 55% of adults aged 65 and older have no dental benefits, fetched 30 July 2026. Dental visits track coverage directly, per the same source.

## §3 - benefit-pillars  [M x3]
required IDs: D-POSITION-009

**Chew on that side again.** A crown with enough tooth underneath it stays where it was put. That is the whole point of the procedure.

**Keep the tooth you still have.** Exposing more of the tooth can make a crown workable where the alternative was taking the tooth out.

**See the tooth, not the gum.** Where gum covers too much of a front tooth, reshaping the margin evens out what shows when you smile.
