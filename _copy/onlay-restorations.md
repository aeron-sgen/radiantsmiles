## §2 - pain-it-kills  [M]
required IDs: D-INCUMBENT-007

A cusp is one of the raised points on the chewing surface of a back tooth. They take the load, so they are what tends to snap. It can happen on something ordinary, like bread.

When a cusp goes, the reflex answer is a full crown. A crown works, but the sound part of the tooth has to be cut down to make room for it. You lose good structure to fix a broken piece.

An onlay covers and rebuilds the cusp instead. It is the option in the middle, and this is the case it exists for.

Waiting is not always a decision about the tooth. It can be a decision about money. The ADA Health Policy Institute reports that 55% of adults aged 65 and older have no dental benefits, fetched 30 July 2026. Dental visits track coverage directly.

## §3 - benefit-pillars  [M x3]
required IDs: D-POSITION-009

**Keep the healthy part of the tooth.** The onlay covers the damaged cusp. The rest of your tooth is left alone rather than reduced for a crown.

**Get back to eating on that side.** The onlay is bonded in place and adjusted to your bite. Chewing should stop being something you plan around.

**See the choice before it is made.** We show you the crack and the digital X-ray. Filling, onlay and crown each cost a different amount of tooth, and you get told which is which.

## §4 - capability-breakdown  [M each]
required IDs: D-OFFER-017

**We find out how far the crack goes.** A clinician examines the cusp and the surrounding tooth. Digital X-rays are taken where they add something. A crack that reaches the root changes the plan, and you are told if it does.

**We remove what has failed.** The broken material and any decay under it come out. What is left gets shaped so the onlay can sit over the cusp and grip properly.

**The onlay is made to your tooth.** It is made to an impression of the prepared tooth. It is not a stock part adjusted to fit.

**We seat it, bond it and balance the bite.** You bite down, and a few adjustments may be necessary before your bite is comfortable. That step is worth doing slowly, because a high onlay is what cracks next.

**We watch the tooth after that.** Repaired cusps get checked at recall, along with the bite that broke the first one.

*Image slot:* the cracked cusp on the operatory screen, and a digital X-ray of the same tooth. Also the prepared tooth and the finished onlay before seating.

## §8 - faq  [S each]
required IDs: D-INCUMBENT-009

**What does an onlay cost?**
We quote it once we have seen how much of the tooth is gone. We do not publish treatment prices. A figure quoted before the examination would only change after it.

**Does insurance pay for it?**
Coverage depends on your plan and on how the restoration is coded. Named plans are listed in full on our insurance and financing page. Network status varies by office and by plan. Contact the office you want with your plan details before you book.

**Does it hurt?**
The tooth is numbed before the preparation starts. Pressure and vibration can still be felt while we work, because numbing does not remove those. If anything feels sharp, say so and we stop.

**Why not just do the crown?**
Sometimes a crown is the right call, and we will say so. An onlay is offered when enough sound tooth remains to be worth keeping. The decision is made at the tooth, not in advance.
