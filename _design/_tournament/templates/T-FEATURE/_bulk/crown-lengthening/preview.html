Crown Lengthening in Las Vegas

Reshaping the gum, and the bone if it is needed, so there is enough tooth left standing for a crown to hold.

Book an appointment Call the office

Sometimes a tooth breaks off close to the gum. Sometimes decay runs in under the edge of it.

Either way, what is left above the gumline is too short to hold a crown.

A crown needs a rim of solid tooth to grip. Without that rim it works loose, leaks at the margin, or comes away in food.

Crown lengthening buys that rim back. In some cases reshaping the gum exposes enough tooth. In other cases a small amount of bone is removed as well.

Coverage is the other half of the picture.

The ADA Health Policy Institute reports that 55% of adults aged 65 and older have no dental benefits, fetched 30 July 2026. Dental visits track coverage directly, per the same source.

Chew on that side again.

A crown with enough tooth underneath it stays where it was put. That is the whole point of the procedure.

Keep the tooth you still have.

Exposing more of the tooth can make a crown workable where the alternative was taking the tooth out.

See the tooth, not the gum.

Where gum covers too much of a front tooth, reshaping the margin evens out what shows when you smile.

We measure before anything is done.

A clinician checks how much tooth sits above the bone, and where the crown margin would need to sit. Digital X-rays are taken where they change the answer.

We reshape the gum, and the bone if it is needed.

The area is numbed with local anaesthetic first. Neighbouring teeth are usually reshaped too, so the new line evens out. Our own SIROlaser page lists crown lengthening among that device's applications. The same page states we review your case to see if you are a candidate.

We let it settle.

The gum needs time to firm up before an impression is worth taking. Our own page puts complete healing of the surgical site at approximately two to three months. Rushing that step is how a crown ends up sitting at the wrong height.

We fit the crown afterwards.

Our own crowns page states that a dental laboratory fabricates the crown. The practice that reshaped the gum is the practice that fits it. Nobody is guessing at where the margin was meant to fall.

Crown lengthening is periodontal work. The crown that follows it is restorative work.

Under a referral model those two stages sit with two separate practices, in two buildings.

Here it is one practice. We run seven offices across Las Vegas, North Las Vegas and Henderson, with one clinical roster.

That is a structural point, not a claim about anyone else. A referral network, or a separately owned group, cannot promise the same continuity, because the next clinician is not on the same roster.

The clinicians on that roster are named. Dr. Adrian Ruiz took his DDS at UCLA in 1995, and Dr. Norma Miranda hers at UCLA in 1976. Both are recorded on our own team page, fetched 30 July 2026.

What does crown lengthening cost?

We quote it after an examination, because the fee depends on how many teeth are involved. We do not publish treatment prices. A figure given before we have looked would only change afterwards.

Will my insurance cover it?

Coverage depends on your plan and on how the work is coded. Network status varies by office and by plan. Contact the office you want with your plan details before you book.

Does it hurt?

The area is numbed with local anaesthetic before anything is done. Our own page states that prescriptions may be provided for pain medication. Ask your dentist anything you are unsure of.
