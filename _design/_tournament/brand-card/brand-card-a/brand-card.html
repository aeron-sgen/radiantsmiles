Radiant Smiles Dental Care

One dental team, seven offices, six specialties under one roof. The identity is built on a light canvas the way the practice's own site already reads, with deep navy carrying the authority and the yellow rationed to the one job it already does. The canvas is warm stone, not a grey: the ramp is derived, not borrowed from a page builder.

02 Palette and roles

Two axes and one spark

C6 recorded the colours in use but assigned no roles. Concept A assigns them. The navy is the COOL axis and it lives in ink and structure now, not in fills. The paper, the card and the alternate band are the WARM axis, a stone ramp derived from the third tone below. The yellow is the spark, cut down to ticks, 2px caps and underlines, with exactly one full-strength moment left on the sheet.

Why the ramp changed. A census of the live homepage markup counts #17274f 93 times, #ffffff 36, #fce003 36, #000000 9, and then #f6f6f6, #c4c4c4 and #d6d6d6: the stock Elementor grey ramp, which belongs to no brand. The first version of this concept answered that with #f7f9fc, a cool blue-grey from the same cold family, and carried no third tone at all. Every neutral on the sheet is now warm stone at hue 30 to 36, and nothing is #ffffff. Every ratio below was computed with the WCAG 2.1 luminance formula, with translucent layers composited to an opaque hex first. The bar is AAA with headroom, not AAA by a rounding. The lowest text ratio actually rendered anywhere on this sheet is now 7.30:1 - the clay ink step on the stone band - and it was found by RENDERING the sheet and reading computed styles, not by reading the stylesheet. The previous solve put that same lowest ratio at 7.0122:1. It PASSED, and it was still the wrong answer: a margin of 0.0122 of a point is a rounding. Three tokens were re-solved a second time for distance from the bar rather than for the bar, each holding its hue and chroma and moving only in value, so no relationship in the palette shifted. Two of the moves were invisible to a source read: an inline style="color:var(--ds-signature)" that no CSS grep caught, and the accent ink, whose true worst ground is a --ds-grid intersection under the masthead eyebrow - a ::before layer that getComputedStyle cannot see at all. That one was solved against a screenshot pixel (#f3efec), not against the paper. Across both passes: the secondary ink from 6.84:1 to 7.73:1 to 8.11:1, the accent ink from 6.20:1 to 7.88:1 on the offer wash and 7.56:1 on that masthead grid intersection, the input placeholder from 5.31:1 to 7.40:1, and the clay from a tone that set type at 6.24:1 to a tone that sets surfaces plus a derived ink step at 7.98:1. An earlier version's placeholder measured 2.95:1 and failed AA outright. The reason for the AAA bar is not perfectionism: contrast sensitivity falls with age independently of acuity, so for the audience C7 names it is the single largest legibility factor.

The anchoring band

Same tokens, one surface flip

C7 measured this brand light dominant at dark_ratio 0.154, with the dark area concentrated in the navy offer band and the footer. That is the only place the brand goes dark, so the identity carries one dark surface, not a dark theme. Adding the surface-dark class re-points the same token names, so a card, a button or an input drops into the band and re-skins itself with nothing restyled by hand. The near-white it flips to is the WARM one, so the band belongs to the same ramp as the paper rather than being a second, colder palette. What this ramp is tuned for, and what it refuses to become

C7 section 4 names anxious and older patients as an audience of this practice and calls legible type a hard rule rather than a preference. The content bible carries the two figures behind that, both from the ADA Health Policy Institute: 55 percent of adults 65 and over and 21 percent of adults 19 to 64 have no dental benefits (Coverage Access and Outcomes, fetched 2026-07-30), so the people most likely to arrive through a published entry price are also the people most likely to be reading with presbyopia. Presbyopia begins around 40 and takes contrast sensitivity down along with acuity, so the answer is not only bigger type, it is darker type. Every floor below is measured, and the sheet holds them without becoming a large-print brochure: the composition, the single accent, the hairline discipline and the ray motif are untouched. Bigger type over more whitespace is what editorial design has always looked like.

The numbers below are read off the RENDER, which is not the same document as the stylesheet. Two things this sheet asserted about itself turned out to be false when it was measured instead of read. Its own type specimen documented a 14px eyebrow while seven of the eleven eyebrows painted at 18px in the secondary grey, because .blk-head p outranks .eyebrow by one point of specificity and the accent ink never reached them. And the masthead claimed a smallest tap target of 48px while one phone specimen sat at 46. Both are closed. Neither was catchable from the source.
